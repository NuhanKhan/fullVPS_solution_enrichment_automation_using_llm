==================================================
EXPERT FIT
==================================================

Pick exactly one:

"strong_fit"
"possible_fit"
"not_fit"

Return "strong_fit" only when all three requirements are satisfied:

1. The patient was incarcerated, detained, jailed, imprisoned, or otherwise in correctional custody when the relevant medical injury or death occurred.
2. The central allegations concern negligent, delayed, denied, inadequate, or improper medical care—or wrongful death allegedly caused by such medical care.
3. A physician experienced in prison or correctional medicine would be appropriate as the primary medical expert on the central standard-of-care and causation issues.

Cases that may qualify include allegations involving:

- Inadequate intake screening or medical assessment
- Failure to recognize a serious medical condition
- Delayed physician evaluation
- Failure to diagnose or treat illness
- Failure to provide or continue necessary medication
- Failure to monitor a serious or deteriorating condition
- Failure to refer or transfer the patient to a hospital
- Inadequate emergency response
- Improper management of chronic disease
- Inadequate management of withdrawal or overdose
- Failure to respond medically to self-harm or suicide risk
- Inadequate care coordination within a correctional facility
- Death allegedly caused by delayed, denied, or inadequate correctional medical care

Return "not_fit" when any of the following applies:

- The person was not incarcerated or detained when the alleged injury occurred.
- The case does not involve medical negligence, inadequate medical care, or medically based wrongful death.
- The central issue is excessive force, assault, restraint, transport, premises liability, classification, security, discipline, or another nonmedical custodial issue.
- The case concerns only correctional policies or constitutional law without a meaningful clinical standard-of-care question.
- The principal expert must instead be a surgeon, psychiatrist, psychologist, dentist, radiologist, obstetrician, cardiologist, oncologist, toxicologist, emergency physician, or another specialist, and correctional-medicine expertise would be secondary.
- The allegations concern suicide prevention primarily from a mental-health, psychological, or security perspective rather than clinical correctional medicine.
- A correctional-medicine physician might provide background testimony but would not be an appropriate primary expert.
- The medical event occurred before incarceration and the complaint does not allege negligent correctional medical care that worsened the condition.

Return "possible_fit" only when the complaint lacks enough facts to determine:

- Whether the patient was incarcerated at the relevant time;
- Whether a medical failure caused the alleged injury or death; or
- Whether a correctional-medicine physician would be the appropriate primary expert.

The fact that a jail, prison, sheriff, department of corrections, or correctional healthcare contractor is named does not automatically make the case a fit.

The fact that an injury or death occurred in custody does not automatically make the case a medical-malpractice or correctional-medicine case.

FIT REASON
==================================================

Write one concise sentence that expressly answers all three questions:

1. Was the patient incarcerated or detained when the medical event occurred?
2. Is the case principally based on medical negligence or medically based wrongful death?
3. Would a prison-medicine physician be an appropriate primary expert?

Example of a strong_fit:

"The decedent was detained in a county jail when medical staff allegedly failed to evaluate worsening respiratory symptoms or arrange emergency treatment, resulting in death, making a correctional-medicine physician an appropriate primary expert on clinical standard of care and causation."

Example of a non-fit involving nonmedical conduct:

"Although the plaintiff was incarcerated, the alleged injury resulted from excessive force rather than inadequate medical treatment, so a correctional-medicine physician would not be the appropriate primary expert."

Example of a non-fit requiring a different specialty:

"The inmate allegedly suffered complications from a specialized surgical procedure, and the principal standard-of-care issue requires a surgeon rather than a correctional-medicine physician."

Example of a possible_fit case:

"The complaint indicates that the plaintiff was incarcerated but does not describe the alleged medical failure or resulting injury sufficiently to determine whether a correctional-medicine physician would be the appropriate primary expert."

This field must never be null.

==================================================
CONFIDENCE SCORE
==================================================

Return an integer from 0 through 100 representing confidence in the expert-fit classification.

- 90–100: Custodial status, medical negligence, outcome, and the correctional-medicine expert role are clearly established.
- 70–89: The case appears to meet or fail the criteria, but some medical details are limited.
- 40–69: Material facts about custody, medical negligence, causation, or expert specialty are unclear.
- 0–39: The complaint provides very little usable information.

The score measures confidence in the classification, not the seriousness of the alleged injury.
